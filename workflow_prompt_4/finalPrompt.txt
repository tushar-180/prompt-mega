The routes worth weighing are a rollback, a configuration change, turning a feature off, an isolated hotfix and a scheduled permanent fix. A route is out whatever else is in its favour where the evidence shows it would lose data, put an older security hole back, break a customisation another live client runs on, lean on a migration nothing has tested, lean on a dependency that is not available or need access nobody in this run has.

Every incident ends the run carrying exactly 1 of these 7 recovery readiness statuses, never 2 and never none, and where more than 1 fits the one higher up the list wins. Emergency Release Candidate is where a route survives every test, the runbook emergency criteria are met, and the incident reads Sev1 or Sev2 in PagerDuty or carries a confirmed risk of permanent data loss or a confirmed security exposure. Scheduled Release Candidate is everything else with a clean route that can wait. Workaround Only is where the workaround in place is safe and holding but the permanent fix has no tested rollback behind it. No Technical Release Required is where recovery rests on the client or a third party and nothing has to ship from here. Hold Missing Technical Evidence is where test, rollback, migration, dependency or production version evidence does not exist or could not be read even after Chrome below had been tried. Hold Unsafe Recovery Route is where no route survives the tests above. Leadership Risk Acceptance Required is where a route exists but only by accepting material data loss, a security regression or an impact on another live client.
 
Only an Emergency Release Candidate competes for capacity, but every incident gets a Priority Score out of 100 recorded against it, built by adding 40 for an active full service outage confirmed in PagerDuty, 25 for a confirmed risk of permanent data loss, 20 for a confirmed security exposure, 10 where the affected user count is 1000 or above or 6 where it is 250 through 999 or 3 where it is 25 through 249 or 0 below 25, and 5 where the workaround expires on or before 2026-09-06. That score is for people to read and is not what decides the order. The order runs off this chain, read left to right and stopping at whichever test separates 2 incidents: an active full service outage, a confirmed risk of permanent data loss, a confirmed security exposure, the larger affected user count, the sooner workaround expiry, the earlier incident start timestamp, and a tie still standing goes to the alphabetically smaller Incident ID.
