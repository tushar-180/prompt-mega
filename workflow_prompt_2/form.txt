1. Your workflow prompt *

Work 1 live tender end to end, and finish with a Bid, a Conditional Bid or a No Bid that the bid owner can sit down with and check line by line against what the buyer actually published. Today is 2026-09-01, Asia/Kolkata. Treat that as fixed, because every date window and every deadline comparison below is measured off it. A No Bid with the evidence stacked behind it is a good result. Getting to a bid is not the win condition here, so don't let the analysis quietly drift that way.

The tender comes off a Google Sheet called Tender Tracker 2026, which sits in My Drive under Tender Management, then 2026. The tab you want is Pending Tenders. Row 1 is just headers, so ignore it and start at row 2. Going left to right, the columns are Tender Name, Buyer Name, Tender Reference, Status, Submission Deadline, Contract Start Date, Contract Duration, Bid Owner, Bid Owner Email, Priority, Drive Folder, Portal URL, Decision and Decision Date. Out of everything sitting on that tab you are taking exactly 1 row, and you work that 1 through to the end. A row is only a candidate in the first place if Status says Pending Review, the Decision cell is still empty, and the Submission Deadline falls between 2026-09-01 and the end of that calendar year with both of those dates counting, Asia/Kolkata. Sort whatever survives by deadline, earliest at the top. If 2 rows share a deadline, Priority settles it, High over Medium over Low, and if they are still level after that, Tender Reference A to Z. Don't come back asking which one to take. The sort has already answered that. And if the filter empties the list completely, write nothing anywhere, just say in the closing message how many rows you read and what knocked each one out.

The tender pack wants reading in full, not the first 2 documents and a skim of the rest. The folder path is sitting in that row's Drive Folder cell. These folders live in My Drive under Tenders, then the contract year, then the buyer, then a folder that opens with the tender reference and closes with the short tender name, so a cargo tracking tender for the Coastal Ports Authority sits at Tenders/2027/Coastal Ports Authority/CPA-CTS-2027-031 Cargo Tracking System. That tail end, Cargo Tracking System, is the short tender name, and it comes back in every Jira issue, Notion page and email subject below. That tender is made up, and so is every example below that uses it. The reference you work with comes off the Tender Reference cell, character for character, and the short name off the folder you opened. Prove you are in the right folder before you read a word of it: at least 1 document inside has to print that reference exactly as the cell has it. Right buyer but the reference doesn't match? Wrong folder, so write the mismatch down and stop work on that tender rather than pulling apart somebody else's pack. Filenames are often numbered in reading order, an RFP main document first and eligibility criteria after it, but often is not always, so read what is in there rather than what the numbering led you to expect. Every workbook gets every one of its tabs read, the compliance one and the pricing one included, and note the filename you actually opened for each, because that filename is what the citations below have to carry.

The buyer's live tender page, at the address in the Portal URL cell, matters as much as the folder does. What you are after is anything published between the issue date printed on the main RFP and 2026-09-01, Asia/Kolkata: addenda, corrigenda, amended documents, clarification notices and responses, deadline changes, eligibility changes, changed submission instructions. Public notice page only. A notice belongs to this tender only where it carries the tender reference exactly as the Tender Reference cell has it, and that page will be carrying other buyers and other references that don't belong here however close the subject matter reads. If that page asks for a login, or the Portal URL cell is empty and the buyer's public page won't turn up from the buyer name and the tender reference, mark the portal check blocked. Everything a notice could have moved becomes Cannot Determine, and the decision caps at Conditional Bid however clean the rest of the pack looks. No exceptions to that cap. A page that opens without a login and carries nothing for the reference across that window is a different thing altogether: that is a portal check completed with a nil result, so record it as checked with no notices found and carry on. Nothing becomes Cannot Determine on the back of it, and the cap does not fire. Anything needing an account is off limits on this run, Upwork, Freelancer, LinkedIn and Apollo included, along with any portal keeping its notices behind a login.

Portal notice and folder document disagree on a deadline, a requirement, an eligibility rule? Whatever was published last wins. The wording it replaced gets recorded as superseded, both dates spelled out, on the Notion decision page described further down. Take a case, with dates invented here only to show the rule. The main RFP prints a submission deadline of 2030-03-10, and a corrigendum dated 2030-02-14 moves it to 2030-03-17. 2030-03-17 is the authoritative deadline from that point on, in whatever time zone the buyer states it in, and a later corrigendum moving it again would beat that in turn. Where 2 publications carry the same date and contradict each other, don't settle it by taking whichever reading suits us. Record both, mark the requirement Cannot Determine, and put the question on the clarification list.

The effective requirement set gets built from the pack and the portal together. Everything the pack marks mandatory goes in, plus the 5 non-mandatory requirements carrying the most delivery risk, and ties there break on the lower document number, then the lower page number. Each requirement carries 9 things with it. Quote the requirement itself, in the buyer's own words. Say whether it is mandatory. Give the source filename and page number, or the workbook filename with its tab and row. Name the addendum or corrigendum that moved it and the document version it now stands at, or None where nothing moved it. Then the status, which is Compliant, Not Compliant, Partially Compliant or Cannot Determine. Then the company evidence, naming the Notion page and the record you read it off. The action that closes it. A fatal, curable or neither call. And the date that action has to land by. Write sources the way somebody would need them to go and check for themselves, so 04_Technical_Specification.pdf page 14, or Annexure C Compliance Matrix tab Eligibility row 22, using whatever the files in front of you are actually called. Never "the technical document" or "the compliance sheet".

The evidence behind all of that sits in Notion. The Company Knowledge Base teamspace holds the database pages Certifications, Project Experience, Client References and Technical Capabilities. Historical Bid Outcomes is not in there, it lives over in the Bid Management teamspace. A certification is worth nothing to you here unless its record on Certifications carries all 4 of a Certificate Number, an Issue Date, an Expiry Date and a Scope, and that Scope has to cover the thing the tender is actually asking about, not a cousin of it sitting next door. For the contract period, take the Contract Start Date cell and add the Contract Duration cell to it, and that gives you the end date everything gets measured against. Where the tender wants something in force across that whole stretch, a certification or a licence, insurance cover, a staffing level, hold the Expiry Date up against the contract end date and count the gap in months. Say a certificate runs out on 2030-06-30 and the contract runs to 2031-04-30. That is 10 months of contract with nothing covering it, which makes the requirement Partially Compliant, not Compliant, and somebody having a renewal pencilled in doesn't change that. Project references work the same way. Project Type, Customer Type, Contract Value Band and Completion Date on the Project Experience record all have to clear what the tender asks for, and clearing 3 of the 4 is Partially Compliant with the one that failed named. Historical Bid Outcomes is background on this buyer and nothing more. It never decides the bid on its own.

Delivery capacity comes out of Jira, where 2 projects matter: Bid Management, key BID, across its Tender Pipeline, Active Bids, Bid Compliance and Bid Production components, and Delivery, key DEL, on Active Projects, which is where the live client work sits. Search both projects on the tender reference, then again on the short tender name, both taken word for word off the row. A loose phrase off the tender name won't do. Take the team the tender is asking for and set it against issues whose Due date runs past the Contract Start Date cell, reading the Assignee on each one. An issue counts as open unless its status is Done. What you are counting is people already committed to open work, not people who happen to be free on 2026-09-01, because free on the run date tells you nothing about free at contract start. If the workspace can't show the required team free by the contract start date, that's a staffing shortfall, and it needs a shortfall count by role and the earliest date it clears.

Curable is a high bar, and it is meant to be. The record has to name 3 things: what closes the issue, who closes it, and a date on or before the authoritative deadline on the submission side, or on or before the Contract Start Date on the delivery side. Anything short of those 3 is fatal, or it is Cannot Determine. An issue with no named renewal or hiring plan behind it is fatal however fixable it feels. And where the documents, the portal and the company evidence between them don't settle compliance, that is Cannot Determine: say exactly what is missing, and don't read the silence as either a pass or a fail.

1 decision comes out of all this. Just the 1. It is No Bid when a mandatory requirement is Not Compliant and the issue behind it is fatal, and there you write up the requirement, the evidence for the failure, and why it can't be closed in time. It is Bid when every mandatory requirement is Compliant, no staffing shortfall stands at the contract start date, and nothing marked Cannot Determine touches a mandatory requirement. Everything in between is a Conditional Bid, and every condition gets an owner and a date it has to close by.

3 clarification questions, and that is the ceiling. Each one names the requirement it goes at, and why the answer would move eligibility, price, technical scope, staffing or the decision itself. An answer that moves none of those is not worth a question, even if the buyer will take 10. If more than 3 qualify, mandatory requirements come first, then whichever blocks the larger share of the priced scope, then the lower document number.

Here is what Jira should be holding by the end of the run. In Bid Management (BID), on the Bid Compliance component, 1 issue of type Task per mandatory requirement that came out Not Compliant, Partially Compliant or Cannot Determine. Cap that at 8, fatal ranked ahead of curable, ties going to the lower document number and then the lower page number. The summary is the tender reference in square brackets and then the check, so [CPA-CTS-2027-031] Verify the security certification covers the contract period. The Labels field takes the bare reference, CPA-CTS-2027-031, no brackets. Each of those issues carries the requirement quoted from source, the source filename with its page, the compliance status, the fatal or curable call, the thing that closes it, and a Due date in Asia/Kolkata on or before the authoritative deadline. Lay the rest of the issue out however you like. Every clarification question you kept gets its own issue on Bid Compliance too, named and labelled the same way, carrying the question text and the requirement behind it.

Bid Production, same project, ends up with 6 issues, same bracketed prefix and same label, and they run in this order because that is the order the work actually happens in: Complete Technical Proposal, Complete Commercial Proposal, Complete Legal Review, Complete Final Compliance Review, Obtain Management Approval and Final Tender Submission. Their Due dates get counted backwards from the authoritative deadline in working days, so Final Tender Submission sits 2 working days ahead of it, Obtain Management Approval 4, Complete Final Compliance Review 6, Complete Legal Review 8, Complete Commercial Proposal 10, and Complete Technical Proposal right back at 15. Not 1 of those Due dates is allowed to land on a Saturday or a Sunday. That is the schedule when there is room for it. When there isn't, when 2026-09-01 and the authoritative deadline have fewer than 15 working days between them, the earlier issues are the ones that give way. Final Tender Submission keeps its 2 working days clear of the deadline whatever happens, and nothing goes earlier than 2026-09-01. Say which ones you squeezed in the closing message, and by how much.

1 Notion page comes out of it as well, in the Bid Management teamspace, inside the Tender Decisions database. The title is the tender reference and then the short tender name, so CPA-CTS-2027-031 Cargo Tracking System. On it goes the tender name, buyer name and tender reference. The original deadline, and the authoritative deadline with the notice that set it. Contract start and end dates. The effective RFP version, plus every addendum and corrigendum applied and the wording each one superseded. The full requirement set, all 9 things per requirement. Fatal issues, curable issues with owner and close date, and Cannot Determine items with what is missing on each. The clarification questions. The decision, and the reasoning behind it in 200 words or fewer. Then the housekeeping: filenames of every document you read, Jira issue keys and summaries created or updated, and the Gmail draft subject.

The last thing out of the run is a Gmail draft. 1 of them, unsent, sitting in Drafts on the single signed in account, addressed to whatever the Bid Owner Email cell holds. The subject is the bracketed reference, then Bid Decision, then the decision itself, so [CPA-CTS-2027-031] Bid Decision No Bid. Keep the body between 180 and 260 words. It wants the tender name and the buyer, the authoritative deadline and the notice that set it, the decision and the 1 finding that drove it. Then the fatal issues, the curable ones with an owner and a close date against each, and anything still sitting at Cannot Determine. The clarification questions you kept go in as well, and it finishes on the 1 thing the bid owner has to do next, with the date it is due. No attachments. And nothing gets sent from this run, not through a connector and not through a browser. If that Bid Owner Email cell is empty, leave To empty and open the body with the line RECIPIENT NOT CONFIRMED.

The decision itself goes back into the sheet: the Decision cell of that same row on Pending Tenders, and 2026-09-01 in Decision Date. Nothing else on the sheet gets touched, and the row stays on the tab you found it on.

Run this twice on 2026-09-01 and the second run should leave no new mark. So look before you create anything. Run the JQL project = BID AND labels = "CPA-CTS-2027-031" across Bid Compliance and Bid Production, and search the Notion database Tender Decisions and the Gmail Drafts folder for that same bracketed prefix. Anything already carrying it gets updated where it stands rather than made a second time. However many times this runs, 1 tender reference still means 1 decision page, 6 production issues and 1 draft.

All of this assumes a few things are already sitting there: the Google Sheet Tender Tracker 2026 with its Pending Tenders tab, the tender folder in Google Drive under Tenders, the Notion teamspaces Company Knowledge Base and Bid Management with Tender Decisions inside the latter, the Jira project Bid Management (BID) with its Tender Pipeline, Active Bids, Bid Compliance and Bid Production components, the Jira project Delivery (DEL) with its Active Projects component, and 1 signed in Gmail account.

Reach each app through its connected integration where that works, and use the Chrome browser plugin where it doesn't. Missing, erroring, timing out, refusing the call, reporting a tool it doesn't recognise, handing back nothing while the screen clearly shows something, or handing back something the screen contradicts: every one of those means switch. Retry once when a failure looks transient, and wait out a rate limit rather than falling back for it. When the integration and the screen disagree, believe the screen. Nothing here gets called missing, unauthorized, unavailable or blocked until Chrome has been pointed at it and failed too. The login rule further up covers the buyer's tender portal and other third party sites, and has nothing to say about these workspace apps, where the browser is simply how the work gets done when the connector won't.

Only 2 things stop the run outright: the Tender Tracker 2026 sheet failing on both routes, or the tender folder failing on both routes. Report either one as blocked, quoting the exact error text each route handed back. Everything else gets written down and worked around. A document that won't open, a portal page behind a login, a Notion page that isn't there, a missing Jira project or component, an issue or a draft that won't save: name it exactly as this brief names it, say what it cost the analysis, mark the requirements it left as Cannot Determine, and finish every part of the run that doesn't depend on it. Don't invent a file, folder, sheet tab, Notion page, Jira issue, portal notice or certificate, don't report something as opened or created when it wasn't, and don't report an app as unreachable when only 1 of the 2 routes was tried. And don't take the reading of a requirement that lets the company bid just because it lets the company bid.

You are done when the selected row on Pending Tenders carries the decision and 2026-09-01, the Notion page inside Tender Decisions has every field named above either filled in or marked Cannot Determine, the Jira component Bid Compliance holds the compliance issues within the cap of 8 plus 1 issue per clarification question, Bid Production holds its 6 with the back counted Due dates on them, 1 unsent draft sits in Gmail Drafts, and the closing message covers the tender you took and what ruled the other rows out, the authoritative deadline and the notice that set it, requirement counts by compliance status, the fatal issues, the curable ones, the Cannot Determine items, the clarification questions you kept and the ones you dropped with reasons, any production Due dates you had to squeeze and by how much, every step that fell back to the Chrome browser plugin with the error that sent it there, and every step still blocked with the error from both routes.
