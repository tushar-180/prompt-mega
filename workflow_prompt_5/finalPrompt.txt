The plan a claim gets tested against is not whatever the report row shows, it is what the account record says, so open the Salesforce Accounts record on the Account ID off the row and read Current Plan, Pending Plan, Plan Change Effective Date and Contract Add Ons. Account Name is a label people retype, so 2 accounts reading nearly the same name settle on Account ID and never on the spelling. Where Pending Plan is filled with no Plan Change Effective Date beside it, the plan question cannot be settled, so every claim on that call that turns on plan lands on Needs Product Confirmation while the rest of that call still gets worked normally. A product claim is any line in Preparation Notes that says something about the product itself, so a feature existing, what it is called, what it does, whether it is live rather than in testing, whether it is included in the customer's plan, or whether it is going to be shown or promised on the call. Lines about price, discount, contract length, delivery timelines, who is attending, meeting logistics, competitor talk or anything the customer is quoted as saying are not product claims and stay exactly as written, however wrong they look, because that is somebody else's call to make. 1 sentence naming 2 features carries 2 claims and each gets worked on its own, and a feature named twice in the same notes is still 1 claim. Every claim gets pinned to the call it came off and the account behind it, so nothing read on 1 account is ever carried across to another.

Call Type sets the bar a claim has to clear. On a Product Demonstration, anything the notes say will be opened, shown or walked through on screen has to come back with Availability Stage reading Generally Available for that account's plan on that call date, so a feature sitting at Beta, Limited Release or Internal Only is not safe to demo however real it is, and it lands on Beta Only even where the notes never used the word beta. On a Discovery Call nothing is being shown, so a feature at Beta or Limited Release is fine to talk about as long as the corrected wording says which of the 2 it is, and a stage below Generally Available does not on its own move the claim off Current, which needs the name, the plan or the existence of the feature to be actually wrong. Where Preparation Notes is empty, or carries plenty of lines and not 1 product claim among them, that is not a failure and not an unmatched call, so the record still gets its Verification Status and Verified On written and the closing message says the notes held nothing to check.

2 Notion sources settle a claim and both are read only here. The Product Changelog database has Change ID, Feature Name, Change Type, Effective Date, Plans Affected, Changelog Version, Superseded By and Details on it, and the entries that count are the ones with an Effective Date between 2026-03-10 and 2026-12-31 inclusive, plus any entry with no Effective Date on it. That window runs well past the call window on purpose, so a change dated after somebody's call still gets picked up and ruled out rather than never seen at all. The Feature Availability database has Feature Name, Current Official Name, Availability Stage, Plans Included, Add On Required and Last Reviewed Date on it, and that one gets read end to end. Feature Availability says what is true on the day it was last reviewed, Product Changelog says when a change takes hold, and neither of them on its own settles a claim about a call sitting on a date.

Every claim lands on exactly 1 of 7 statuses and never on 2. Current is a claim that is accurate for that account's plan on that call date. Renamed is a feature still there under a different official name. Replaced is a feature that has been taken over by a different feature. Deprecated is a feature on the way out that should not be shown as live. Beta Only is a feature that is real but sits in beta or a limited test. Plan Restricted is a feature that is real and live but is not in that account's plan on that call date. Needs Product Confirmation is where a claim lands when the sources are missing, stale, contradicting each other or simply not enough to settle it, and it is the honest answer rather than the last resort, so a feature the notes name that turns up in neither Notion database lands there and never on Current.

The effective date does most of the work here. A Product Changelog entry only bites where its Effective Date falls on or before the Scheduled Call Date And Time, so a rename, a restriction, a replacement or a removal dated after that call is real but not yet real for that call, and the claim holds as Current with the coming change written into the correction summary rather than flagged against the call. A change already effective on the call date lands its status. An entry carrying no Effective Date settles nothing, and the claim it touches goes to Needs Product Confirmation. Where the 2 of them disagree the claim goes to Needs Product Confirmation, but work out why they disagree before landing that, because if it comes down to a changelog entry dated after the call then they are not really disagreeing, the call date wins and Feature Availability holds. A feature renamed more than once is the same trap twice over, so take the name off Current Official Name in Feature Availability and let the changelog settle only which of those hops had taken effect by the call date. Follow a Superseded By all the way to the end, because stopping at the 1st hop leaves the BDE saying a name that was already old. A feature outside Plans Included is still available where Feature Availability names an Add On Required that shows up in that account's Contract Add Ons, and a claim on it is Current rather than Plan Restricted.

2 more things decide whether a changelog entry touches a call at all. Plans Affected on the entry has to include the plan that call is being tested against, so a restriction that only lands on Starter accounts does nothing to a claim on a Pro account and that claim stays Current, while an entry with Plans Affected empty is taken as landing on every plan. Last Reviewed Date on the Feature Availability row is the other 1, and a row last reviewed before 2026-06-10 is stale, so it cannot settle a claim on its own. Where a stale row is the only thing there is on a feature, the claim goes to Needs Product Confirmation with the row and its Last Reviewed Date named. Where a stale row is backed by a Product Changelog entry already effective on the call date, that entry settles the claim and the row is read only for the name. An entry ruled out by Plans Affected has settled nothing though, so a stale row sitting behind 1 of those is still the only thing there is and the claim goes to Needs Product Confirmation. A row reviewed on or after 2026-06-10 with no changelog entry against the feature at all is enough on its own, and a claim resting on it is Current.

A correction earns a Slack message only where it is material, so a claim landing on Renamed, Replaced, Deprecated or Plan Restricted is material every time, a claim landing on Beta Only is material only where the notes present the feature as generally available or promise to demo it, and a claim landing on Needs Product Confirmation is material only where the notes promise the feature will be shown live or say it is included in the plan.
