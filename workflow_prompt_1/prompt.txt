Open the existing Jira project/space used by the BDE or Sales team for prospect management. Use the existing Jira project and do not create a new project or space. Find the prospect issues in that Jira project and check their current status. Only work on prospects whose status is exactly `Pending`. For this run, take only the first 2 Pending prospects you find and process only those 2. Do not pick all Pending prospects and do not continue to a third prospect after the first two are completed. Keep the original Jira issue for each prospect because that same issue must be updated after the research is finished.

For each of the two selected Pending prospects, open the Jira issue and first understand all the information already available there. Get the company name, website, industry, location, contact information, assigned BDE, notes and any other useful information from the issue. Use the company name and website from the Jira issue when starting the research. Do not guess missing company information. If something is not available in Jira, research it through approved public sources only.

Open the company's website and properly understand the business before deciding what opportunity exists. Review what the company sells, what services or products it provides, who it appears to target, which markets it serves and how the company currently presents itself online. Check the website experience from a customer's point of view, including the homepage, navigation, service or product pages, calls to action, contact options, trust signals, content, mobile experience, overall design and conversion opportunities. Look for practical issues that could affect the company's ability to attract visitors or convert them into enquiries.

Use Google Search to research the company's public online presence. Search for the company, its main services or products, relevant commercial searches, important locations, competitors and other relevant queries. Look at what appears in Google results and compare the prospect's visibility with relevant competitors. Look for SEO opportunities, content gaps, search visibility issues and areas where competitors appear to be performing better. Do not claim to have Google Search Console data unless an actual connected Google Search Console account or property is available and can be accessed. If Search Console is not available, treat the SEO findings as public Google Search research and make that clear in the report.

Use Google PageSpeed Insights to check the company's website performance. Review the available mobile and desktop results, performance score, Core Web Vitals, loading performance and the main recommendations shown by PageSpeed Insights. Use these findings to identify website performance or technical opportunities that could affect user experience, SEO or conversions. Do not invent PageSpeed scores or technical findings. If PageSpeed Insights cannot be opened through the available tools, use the Chrome browser plugin to open it and continue the analysis. The Chrome browser plugin should also be used whenever a company website, Google result, public profile or other required webpage cannot be properly opened through the normal connected application.

Research the company's relevant competitors using Google Search and the available public sources. Choose competitors that actually target similar customers, products, services or markets rather than random companies from the same industry. Compare the prospect with those competitors wherever useful, especially around website experience, SEO visibility, content, search presence, social activity, calls to action, conversion experience, website performance and publicly observable advertising activity. The purpose is to understand what the competitors are doing better and where the prospect has a realistic opportunity to improve. Only use information that can actually be verified. If something cannot be verified, do not make it up.

Review the company's public social-media presence when relevant. Check whether the company is active, whether it posts consistently, what type of content it publishes and whether its social presence appears weaker or stronger than relevant competitors. Use this only when it helps identify a genuine marketing opportunity.

Once the research is complete, decide what the strongest marketing opportunity is for the company. Match that opportunity with the agency service that makes the most sense. This could be SEO, Paid Advertising, Social Media Marketing, Website Optimization or a combination of services, but do not recommend every service just because the agency offers them. The recommendation should be based on the actual problems found during the research. Explain what the company is lacking, what evidence supports the finding, what competitors are doing better and how the agency could realistically help.

Give the prospect a priority score from 0 to 100 based on the strength of the need, business fit, potential value, competitive gap and quality of the available evidence. The score should have a short explanation so the BDE understands why the prospect is considered high, medium or low priority. For strong prospects, research the appropriate decision-maker through approved public sources. Prefer someone whose role is relevant to the recommended service, such as a Founder, CEO, Marketing Head, Marketing Director, Growth Manager or Business Owner. Do not guess a person's position or contact information. If a decision-maker cannot be reliably verified, mention that it could not be verified.

After the company research is finished, check the connected Gmail account for previous communication with the company or the identified decision-maker. Look for previous conversations, proposals, follow-ups or other relevant sales communication. Use the previous communication when preparing the new outreach so the BDE does not receive a generic message that ignores an existing relationship. If no relevant conversation is found, record that there was no previous Gmail interaction found.

Create a detailed research report for each prospect and save it in the existing Google Drive folder named `Sales Prospect Research`. If that folder already exists, open and use it. Do not create another folder with a similar name. The report filename must follow this exact format: `Prospect_Analysis_[Company_Name]_[YYYY-MM-DD]`. For example, if the company is ABC Technologies and the research is completed on September 1, 2026, the filename should be `Prospect_Analysis_ABC_Technologies_2026-09-01`. Keep the company name clean in the filename and do not use unnecessary symbols.

The report itself should be written as a proper sales research document rather than a collection of raw search results. It should explain the company, the website findings, PageSpeed findings, SEO and Google Search findings, social presence, relevant competitors, what competitors are doing better, the main marketing gaps, the recommended agency service, the reason for that recommendation, the priority score, the decision-maker if verified, the previous Gmail interaction, the recommended sales angle and the next action. A BDE should be able to open the report and immediately understand what is wrong, why it matters, what competitors are doing better, what we should pitch and why the prospect is worth contacting.

After saving the report, open the existing Google Sheet used for prospect tracking. The preferred sheet name is `Sales Prospect Research Tracker`. If a sheet with this name already exists, use it. If the team already has an approved prospect-tracking Google Sheet with a different existing name, use that existing sheet instead of creating a duplicate. Find the existing row for the prospect and update that row rather than creating another row. Add or update the research status, main marketing gap, recommended service, competitor opportunity, priority score, decision-maker, Gmail interaction status, Google Drive report name or link, outreach status and next action. Do not remove useful existing sales information from the sheet.

After the report and sheet have been updated, prepare a personalized outreach email for the prospect in the connected Gmail account. The email must be created as a Gmail Draft only. Never send the email. Never click Send, never schedule it and never queue it for automatic delivery. The BDE must review the draft and decide whether to send it manually. The draft should use the actual research findings and should sound like a real person who has taken the time to understand the company. Do not write a generic agency introduction. Explain naturally what we noticed, what the company appears to be lacking, what relevant competitors are doing better and how our recommended service could help address the opportunity. Keep the email professional, conversational and reasonably short. Do not make guaranteed claims about rankings, traffic, leads or revenue.

Use the Gmail draft subject and identification format `Prospect Outreach - [Company Name]`. After creating the email, verify that it is saved in Gmail Drafts. The outreach status must be recorded as `Draft Ready — Not Sent`. Do not mark the prospect as Contacted, Emailed or Outreach Sent because the email has not been sent. If the Gmail connection cannot create a draft, do not try to send the email through another method. Instead, save the completed email copy inside the research report and record the outreach status as `Draft Preparation Required`.

Once the report is saved, the Google Sheet is updated and the Gmail draft is confirmed, return to the original Jira issue. Update the existing issue with the important findings, including the recommended service, priority score, main marketing opportunity, competitor opportunity, Google Drive report name or link, decision-maker if verified, Gmail status and recommended next action. Then change the Jira issue from `Pending` to the existing Jira status that represents completed research or outreach readiness. Use the team's existing status such as `Research Completed` or `Outreach Ready` if one is available. Do not create a new Jira status and do not mark the issue as Contacted because the Gmail message has only been drafted.

Use the following existing resource names consistently throughout the workflow: the existing BDE/Sales Jira project or space for prospect issues, the existing `Sales Prospect Research Tracker` Google Sheet for tracking, the existing `Sales Prospect Research` Google Drive folder for reports, the report filename format `Prospect_Analysis_[Company_Name]_[YYYY-MM-DD]`, and the Gmail draft format `Prospect Outreach - [Company Name]`. Do not create duplicate Jira projects, duplicate Google Drive folders, duplicate tracking sheets or duplicate reports when the correct existing resource is already available.

If any website, Google page, PageSpeed page or public source cannot be opened using the normal connected tools, use the Chrome browser plugin and continue the research from there. Do not stop simply because one application cannot open a page. At the same time, never pretend that something was checked when it was not actually accessible. Keep every finding based on information that can be verified from Jira, the connected applications or approved public sources.

The most important rule is that this workflow processes only 2 Pending Jira prospects per run. It researches those two prospects properly, saves their reports in `Sales Prospect Research`, updates the `Sales Prospect Research Tracker` sheet, creates personalized Gmail drafts with the status `Draft Ready — Not Sent`, updates the original Jira issues and then stops. Do not process a third Pending prospect and do not send any email automatically.
