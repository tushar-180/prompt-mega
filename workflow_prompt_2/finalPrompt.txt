Treat today as 2026-09-01, Asia/Kolkata. Every date window, every age calculation, every deadline comparison below runs off that. A No Bid with the evidence behind it is a good outcome. The tender comes off a Google Sheet, Tender Tracker 2026. It's in My Drive under Tender Management, then 2026. Go to the Pending Tenders tab. Row 1 holds the headers, so your data starts at row 2. Sort what's left by deadline, earliest first. On a tie Priority settles it, High over Medium over Low, and if that still ties, Tender Reference A to Z. Don't ask which one to take. Then read the tender pack, all of it. The folder path is in that row's Drive Folder cell. Right buyer, wrong reference? Wrong folder. Record the mismatch and stop work on that tender instead of analysing somebody else's pack. Filenames are numbered in reading order and usually go 01_RFP_Main_Document.pdf, 02_Eligibility_Criteria.pdf, and so on down the pack. Usually. It's a naming convention rather than a guarantee, so read whatever is actually in there. Next, the buyer's live tender page, at the address in the Portal URL cell. You're looking for anything published between the issue date printed on the main RFP and 2026-09-01, Asia/Kolkata. Addenda, corrigenda, amended documents, clarification notices and responses, deadline changes, eligibility changes, changed submission instructions. Public notice page only. If that page wants a login, or the Portal URL cell is empty and the buyer's public page won't turn up from the buyer name and the tender reference, mark the portal check blocked. Everything a notice could have moved becomes Cannot Determine, and the decision caps at Conditional Bid however clean the rest of the pack looks. No exceptions to that cap. The notices are where deadlines and eligibility rules actually move, so a pack read without them may already be out of date. Anything needing an account is off limits for this run, Upwork, Freelancer, LinkedIn and Apollo included, along with any portal keeping its notices behind a login.

Portal notice and folder document disagree on a deadline, a requirement, an eligibility rule? Latest official publication wins. The wording it replaced gets recorded as superseded, both dates spelled out, on the Notion decision page described further down. Say 01_RFP_Main_Document.pdf prints a submission deadline of 2026-09-20, and Corrigendum 02, dated 2026-08-25, moves it to 2026-09-25. The authoritative deadline is then 2026-09-25, in whatever time zone the buyer states, and a later corrigendum moving it again would beat that. Where 2 publications share a date and contradict each other you have a different problem. Don't resolve those by picking the friendlier reading. Record both, mark the requirement Cannot Determine, and add the question to the clarification list.

Now build the effective requirement set, from the pack and the portal together. Everything the pack marks mandatory goes in, plus the 10 non-mandatory requirements carrying the most delivery risk. Ties there break on the lower document number, then the lower page number. There are 11 things to record against each requirement: the requirement quoted from source, whether it's mandatory, the source filename and page number (or workbook filename, tab and row), the document version, the addendum or corrigendum that changed it or None if nothing did, a status of Compliant, Not Compliant, Partially Compliant or Cannot Determine, the company evidence with the Notion page and record you read it from, the risk in a sentence, the action that closes it, a fatal / curable / neither call, and the date that action has to land by. Write sources like 03_Technical_Requirements.pdf page 14, or 07_Annexure_B_Technical_Compliance.xlsx tab Mandatory Requirements row 22. Never "the technical document" or "the compliance sheet".

Company evidence is in Notion. The Company Knowledge Base teamspace has the database pages Certifications, Project Experience, Client References and Technical Capabilities; the Bid Management teamspace has Historical Bid Outcomes. For a certification to count, its record on Certifications needs a Certificate Number, an Issue Date, an Expiry Date and a Scope, and that Scope has to genuinely cover what the tender is asking for. Contract period comes from the Contract Start Date cell plus the Contract Duration cell. Say the tender wants a certification, licence, insurance cover or staffing level valid across that whole period. Compare Expiry Date against contract end date, and write the uncovered gap down in months. Certificate expires 2028-12-15, contract runs to 2029-10-31: that's 10 months uncovered, which makes it Partially Compliant, not Compliant, whatever renewal somebody has planned. Project references work the same way. Project Type, Customer Type, Contract Value Band and Completion Date on the Project Experience record all have to clear what the tender asks. Clear 3 out of 4 and it's Partially Compliant, with the one that failed named. Historical Bid Outcomes is background on this buyer, nothing more. It never decides the bid by itself.

Delivery capacity comes out of Jira, and 2 projects matter: Bid Management, key BID, across its Tender Pipeline, Active Bids, Bid Compliance and Bid Production components, and Delivery, key DEL, on Active Projects, which is where the live client work sits. Search on the tender reference and the short tender name, project in (BID, DEL) AND text ~ "ABC-HMS-2026-001". Loose words like hospital tender won't do. Take the team the tender is asking for and put it against issues whose Due date runs past the Contract Start Date cell. Read the Assignee on each. An issue counts as open unless its status is Done. What you're counting is people already committed to open work, not people who happen to be free on 2026-09-01, because free on the run date tells you nothing about free at contract start. If the workspace can't show the required team free by the contract start date, that's a staffing shortfall, and it needs a shortfall count by role and the earliest date it clears.

Curable is a high bar. The record has to name 3 things: what closes it, who closes it, and a date on or before the authoritative deadline (submission side) or on or before the Contract Start Date (delivery side). Anything less is fatal, or it's Cannot Determine. An issue with no named renewal or hiring plan behind it is fatal, however fixable it feels. And where the documents, the portal and the company evidence together don't settle compliance, that's Cannot Determine: say exactly what's missing, and don't read the silence as either a pass or a fail.

Only 1 decision comes out of all this. Just the 1. It's No Bid when a mandatory requirement is Not Compliant and the issue behind it is fatal, and you write up the requirement, the evidence for the failure, and why it can't be closed in time. It's Bid when every mandatory requirement is Compliant, no staffing shortfall stands at the contract start date, and nothing marked Cannot Determine touches a mandatory requirement. Everything in between is a Conditional Bid, with each condition set against an owner and the date it has to close by.

You get 3 clarification questions, maximum. Each one names the requirement it goes at, and why the answer would move eligibility, price, technical scope, staffing or the decision itself. An answer that moves none of those isn't worth a question, even if the buyer allows 10. If more than 3 qualify, mandatory requirements come first, then whichever blocks the larger share of the priced scope, then the lower document number.
