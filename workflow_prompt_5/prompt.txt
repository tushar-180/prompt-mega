Open Salesforce first and open the `Upcoming Sales Calls` report. Review the upcoming scheduled discovery and product-demonstration calls for the next 7 days. Only process calls that are scheduled, have an assigned BDE, and have a linked customer account. Do not process cancelled, completed, or unassigned calls.

For each eligible call, open the related record from the Salesforce `Accounts` section. Record the exact customer account name, scheduled call date and time, assigned BDE, and customer's current plan. Keep these details connected to the call while processing it so information from one customer is never applied to another customer.

After checking Salesforce, open Notion and open the `Sales Call Preparation` database. Find the preparation record that matches the same customer account and scheduled call. Read the preparation notes and identify every statement that makes a product-related claim. This includes claims about product features, feature names, feature availability, product capabilities, beta features, plan availability, or anything the BDE is planning to demonstrate or promise to the customer.

For every product claim, open the Notion `Product Changelog` database or page and check the latest relevant product changes. Then open the Notion `Feature Availability` database and verify the current availability of the feature. Use the customer's plan from Salesforce when checking plan restrictions. Do not assume that a feature is available to the customer simply because the feature exists in the product.

Classify every claim using exactly one of these statuses: `Current`, `Renamed`, `Replaced`, `Deprecated`, `Beta Only`, `Plan Restricted`, or `Needs Product Confirmation`.

Use `Current` when the claim is accurate for the customer's plan and the scheduled call date. Use `Renamed` when the feature still exists but its official product name has changed. Use `Replaced` when the original feature has been replaced by another feature. Use `Deprecated` when the feature is being removed or should no longer be presented as an active feature. Use `Beta Only` when the feature is available only as a beta or limited testing feature. Use `Plan Restricted` when the feature exists but is not included in the customer's current plan. Use `Needs Product Confirmation` when the available information is missing, conflicting, outdated, or not sufficient to safely verify the claim.

Always check the effective date of every relevant product change. Compare the effective date from the Notion `Product Changelog` with the actual scheduled call date from Salesforce. If a feature will be removed, renamed, restricted, or changed after the scheduled call, do not incorrectly flag the claim for that call. If the change is already effective on the scheduled call date, apply the new status. If no effective date is available and the information cannot be verified confidently, use `Needs Product Confirmation` rather than guessing.

When a claim is incorrect or needs clarification, update the matching record in the Notion `Sales Call Preparation` database. Correct the wording so the BDE can use it safely during the customer call. Keep the corrected wording simple, accurate, and suitable for a sales conversation. Add the relevant supporting evidence or reference from the Notion `Product Changelog` or `Feature Availability` information where the database supports it. Do not create information that is not present in the verified product sources.

If a claim is already correct, leave it unchanged. Do not rewrite preparation notes unnecessarily. Only make changes when the product information requires a correction or clarification.

If a material correction is required, open Slack and use the `#sales-call-alerts` channel to notify the assigned BDE identified in Salesforce. The message should clearly mention the customer account, scheduled call, incorrect claim, and corrected information. Keep the notification short and practical so the BDE can understand the correction before the call.

Before sending a Slack notification, check whether the same correction has already been reported for the same customer call and the same Product Changelog version. Do not send the same notification repeatedly. If the workflow runs again and there is no new relevant change, do not send another alert. If a newer version of the `Product Changelog` introduces a new material correction for the same call, send a new Slack notification only for that new correction.

Do not create Jira issues, Jira tickets, or records in any other application. This workflow uses only Salesforce, Notion, and Slack. Salesforce is used for upcoming calls, account information, assigned BDEs, and customer plans. Notion is used for `Sales Call Preparation`, `Product Changelog`, and `Feature Availability`. Slack is used for verified material corrections.

If Salesforce, Notion, or Slack cannot be opened through the connected application, use the Chrome plugin as the fallback. When using Chrome, open the web version of the same application and locate the exact named resource. For Salesforce, use the `Upcoming Sales Calls` report and related `Accounts` records. For Notion, use the `Sales Call Preparation`, `Product Changelog`, and `Feature Availability` resources. For Slack, use the `#sales-call-alerts` channel. Do not switch to an unrelated page or make up a resource name.

If the required application or exact resource still cannot be accessed through the Chrome fallback, stop processing that particular call and report which application and named resource could not be accessed. Do not guess missing information and do not mark a claim as correct without verification.

Process each call independently. Do not mix preparation notes, customer plans, BDE information, or product claims between different accounts. At the end, make sure each processed call has been checked against the latest relevant product information, corrected preparation notes have been saved in the `Sales Call Preparation` database, and only required material corrections have been sent through the `#sales-call-alerts` Slack channel.
